**Submission Deadline - 72 Hours**

## Build a Tiny Visual Toy

We want to see how you think when the brief is intentionally open.

Your task is simple:

Build a small interactive visual toy using Paper Shaders, or anything else you like.

That's it.

It can be useful, useless, playful, weird, beautiful, experimental, or all of the above.

We care less about how much you build and more about the choices you make.

### Starting point

If you want one, check out:

[Paper Shaders](https://github.com/paper-design/shaders)

You can use it as-is, combine multiple shaders, modify it, or ignore it completely and build something from scratch.

Use whatever tools help you make the idea in your head real.

### What could you make?

Anything.

You could explore:

- ASCII, dithering, or halftone
- Glitch, distortion, or unusual materials
- Generative typography or graphics
- An interactive visualizer or creative tool
- Something we haven't thought of

These are just prompts.

Surprise us.

## Page 2

### A few constraints

#### 1. Make it interactive

We should be able to play with it.

That interaction could come from controls, mouse movement, dragging, typing, sound, camera input, randomness, physics, or something more interesting.

#### 2. Make it feel finished

It does not need many features.

One really good interaction is better than ten mediocre ones.

Pay attention to the details.

Typography, layout, motion, transitions, controls, performance, and overall feel all matter.

#### 3. Build it for the web

Use whatever stack you are most comfortable with.

React, Next.js, Three.js, WebGL, WebGPU, Canvas, CSS, SVG, GSAP, Framer Motion, shaders, or anything else.

There are no bonus points for using a more complicated stack.

### Time

Try to keep this to roughly 6 hours.

We are not expecting a production-ready application.

Do not spend a weekend polishing it for us.

Part of the challenge is deciding what is worth building with limited time.

If there is more you wanted to explore, tell us about it instead of building everything.

### What to submit

Send us:

- A live link
- Source code
- A short note on what you built
- What you would explore next if you had more time

No presentation deck.

No long case study.

Just show us the thing.

## Page 3

### What we're looking for

#### Taste

Does it feel considered?

Did you make interesting visual choices?

#### Creativity

Did you take the idea somewhere unexpected?

#### Interaction

Is it fun or satisfying to use?

Does the interaction feel intentional?

#### Craft

Do the small details hold up?

#### Engineering

Is the implementation thoughtful?

Does it run well?

#### Judgment

Did you spend your limited time on the right things?

### What we're not looking for

We are not testing how many shader techniques you know.

We are not looking for the most complicated implementation.

We are not looking for someone to build a huge product.

And we are definitely not looking for a giant take-home project.

We want to see what happens when someone with strong design taste and engineering ability gets a blank canvas and a few hours to play.

### The brief, in one sentence

Build a tiny interactive visual experience that you think is genuinely cool.

Make something you'd want to keep playing with.
